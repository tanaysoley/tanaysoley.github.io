How to Write Music

This guide explains the simple text-based language used to create music in this tool.

Song Structure

A song is made up of a title, a tempo, and one or more tracks.

                    
                        title MyFirstSong
                        tempo 120

                        begin instrument_name
                        ... notes ...
                        end

                        begin another_instrument
                        ... notes ...
                        end

Notes

Notes are written using Indian Sargam notation. The standard notes are s, r, g, m, p, d, n.

  • Vikrit (Altered) Swars: Use uppercase for Komal (flat) and Teevra (sharp) notes: R, G, M, D, N.
  • Octaves:
    • Prefix a number for lower octaves (e.g., 1s is Sa in the lower octave).
    • Suffix a number for higher octaves (e.g., s1 is Sa in the higher octave).

Example: 1n s R g m p d N s1

Duration

The duration of a note is based on beats. The default duration is 1 beat.

  • + (sustain): Each plus sign adds one beat to the duration. s+ is 2 beats, s++ is 3 beats.
  • - (halve): Each minus sign halves the duration. s- is a half beat, s-- is a quarter beat.

You can combine them: s+-- is a 2-beat note, halved twice, resulting in a half beat duration (2 / 2 / 2 = 0.5).

Chords and Groups

To play multiple notes at the same time (a chord), enclose them in square brackets [].

Example: [s g p] plays Sa, Ga, and Pa simultaneously.

Repetition

To repeat a section of music, enclose it in curly braces {} followed by a number.

Example: {s r g}4 plays the phrase "s r g" four times in a row.

Putting It All Together

                    
                        title MyHappySong
                        tempo 140

                        begin IndianNotesPiano
                        { [s g p]+ [r m d]+ }2
                        s+ g+ m-- p-- d+
                        end
